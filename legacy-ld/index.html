What FHIR is — and what this site is for

FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) is an HL7 specification for healthcare interoperability. The HL7 FHIR Foundation exists to promote global adoption and implementation of that platform: information, educational materials, tools, websites, and project support so the community can collaborate, align, and expand. This L&D site is a structured classroom on top of that public record.

The problem FHIR addresses

Healthcare records are increasingly digitized. As patients move around the ecosystem, electronic health records must be available, discoverable, and understandable. Automated clinical decision support and other machine processing also require data that is structured and standardized — not only locked in PDFs or proprietary tables.

HL7 has produced healthcare data exchange and information modeling standards for more than twenty years. FHIR is a newer specification based on emerging industry approaches, informed by the requirements, successes, and challenges of HL7 v2, HL7 v3 and the RIM, and CDA. It can be used as a stand-alone exchange standard, and it can be used in partnership with those widely deployed standards.

The design aim

FHIR aims to simplify implementation without sacrificing information integrity. It leverages existing logical and theoretical models to provide a consistent, implementable, rigorous mechanism for exchanging data between healthcare applications. Built-in traceability to the HL7 RIM and other content models keeps alignment with prior HL7 patterns without forcing every implementer to become a RIM expert.

The philosophy is to define a base set of resources that, alone or combined, satisfy the majority of common use cases. Remaining needs are covered by a built-in extension mechanism and by Implementation Guides that constrain the platform for a nation, a network, or a clinical specialty.

Official orientation

fhir.org is the home for FHIR implementers, run by the HL7 FHIR Foundation on behalf of the community. The Foundation is a US 501(c)(3), international in scope, closely affiliated with Health Level Seven International. HL7 maintains the standard; fhir.org focuses on use and implementation.

Start with the specification overview at hl7.org/fhir/overview.html, the resource list and the Patient resource, then return here for guided conversation videos.

Foundation services you will use later: Implementation Guide registry, SMART-on-FHIR app registry, design registry, general application registry, monthly product reports, and conformance testing.

The building blocks you cannot skip

Every FHIR conversation — clinical or technical — comes back to resources, shared datatypes, narrative, references, and a small set of exchange paradigms. Miss any of these and the rest of the specification will feel like a pile of XML.

01 · Resource

All exchangeable content is a resource

Resources share a common way to define and represent content, built from reusable datatypes; a common set of metadata; and a human-readable part. Uses range from CarePlan and DiagnosticReport to MessageHeader and CapabilityStatement.

02 · Composition

Combine resources for a use case

FHIR modeling uses composition. Specific use cases are usually implemented by combining resources through references, then tailoring them with StructureDefinition constraints and a CapabilityStatement of the interfaces an implementation exposes.

03 · Narrative

People can still read the record

Each resource carries narrative text. That is a safety feature: software may ignore an extension it does not understand; a clinician should still be able to read what happened. Formats are JSON and XML, with RDF also specified.

04 · REST

Four paradigms, REST first

Exchange happens via REST, Documents, Messages, and Services. REST is the filing-cabinet model: one cabinet per type, one folder per instance, papers as versions. HTTP GET, POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE, search, history, and operations such as $everything.

05 · Terminology

Codes make data comparable

LOINC, SNOMED CT, RxNorm, ICD, and local systems are bound through CodeSystem, ValueSet, and ConceptMap. Bindings may be required, extensible, preferred, or example — and Implementation Guides tell you which.

06 · Conformance

IGs turn the platform into a contract

The base spec is a platform. National programs and clinical societies publish Implementation Guides that define the real exchange. Registries at fhir.org and registry.fhir.org are the catalog. Test what you claim with CapabilityStatement and connectathon scripts.

Where to start — the official road map

The specification editors tell first-time readers exactly how to enter the material. We follow that order so this L&D program does not invent a competing canon.

1

Skim the resource list

Get a sense of what exists before you memorize any one resource. Notice administrative, clinical, diagnostic, medication, workflow, financial, and infrastructure families.

2

Read Patient end to end

Patient shows what a resource definition looks like: identity, human name, telecom, gender, birthDate, address, photo, contact, communication, general practitioner, managing organization, and link.

3

Background pages

Resource definitions; narrative and references; XML and JSON formats; extensibility. If you already know v2, v3, or CDA, read the relationship pages next so you do not map old habits blindly.

4

Use the header tabs

Many readers miss them. Resources are shown in a concise XML-like view and in detailed descriptions, with mappings, examples, profiles, and design notes. Train yourself to switch tabs.

5

Then write software

The specification is targeted at people who will write the software. It is kept concise on purpose. Ambiguity, alternatives, and future plans live on Confluence, chat, and formal change requests — not always in the normative pages.

Community, registries, and Foundation services

A professional FHIR education does not end at the textbook. These are the same buckets fhir.org puts on its home page, written here as an implementer checklist.

Community support

FHIR Chat channels, HL7 work group participation, and implementer question forums. Formal specification changes go through HL7 Jira. Confluence holds methodology and design discussion that is not itself the conformance source.

Registries

Implementation Guides, the design registry, the SMART-on-FHIR app registry, and the general application registry. The full IG catalog is at registry.fhir.org. New guides are registered by GitHub pull request or by contacting the FHIR Director.

Foundation services

Monthly product reports, conformance testing, and the public explanation of fhir.org and the HL7 FHIR Foundation. Trademark use applications cover community and product use of the FHIR name and flame design.

Services for implementers

Implementation Guides hosted by the Foundation, SMART App Launch as the dominant app pattern, and Bulk Data for population-scale export. These are how “we have an API” becomes “we participate in the ecosystem.”
